\chapter{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:introduction}

The problem-statement chapter defines a scheduler for four synthetic data
centers. Here we use proximal policy optimization (PPO) to choose where
service runs and when and where batch work runs. We ask whether this
scheduler can reduce regional net-load ramps and monthly peaks while
meeting service, completion-window, and site-capacity constraints.

Previous work uses workload flexibility to change when data centers draw
power. Google's Carbon-Aware Computing system shifts flexible work using
forecasts and daily completion requirements \cite{radovanovic2021carbonaware}.
DeepEE applies deep reinforcement learning to joint job scheduling and
cooling control \cite{ran2019deepee}. SustainDC provides a benchmark for
workload, cooling, and battery control using observations, forecasts, and
separate operating metrics \cite{naug2024sustaindc}. These studies inform our
use of workload flexibility and learned control. Our focus is their effect
on regional net-load ramps and peaks; we do not measure carbon savings.

We evaluate the scheduler by four-fold cross-validation over the twelve
months of 2025, as defined in the problem statement: each fold trains three
independently seeded policies on nine months and scores them on the other
three, against the same no-flexibility baseline, \(\mu_{\mathrm{NF}}\), which
executes all work at its origin in its arrival hour. Every 2025 month is
therefore scored by policies that never saw it. The test month, May 2026, is
reserved for the final ten-seed campaign and is not used here. The study's
contribution is the
scheduling model and its evaluation using Google workload traces and EIA
grid data. PPO supplies the learning method. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}
gives the code locations and reproduction commands.

\chapter{THE SCHEDULING METHOD}
\label{sec:scheduler}

One interaction is one decision hour. The scheduler observes the available
grid information and workload, chooses an action, and receives a reward
once the hour's realized net load is known. One episode is a month of 672 to
744 hours on the Google trace's clock (UTC\(-7\)), starting at 07:00 UTC on
its first day. Queues and site-power history carry across midnight;
each month ends with empty queues.

\section{Observation}
\label{subsec:observation}

The policy receives an 82-number observation built from the information
available at hour \(t\). For each market, it includes gross demand and net
load from \(t-1\), the four gross-demand and four net-load forecasts issued
at \(t-1\), the steepest forecast climb for each quantity, and the running
adjusted and original peaks. For each site, it includes current service and
batch arrivals, queued batch work, and the previous hour's power. Five
queue-urgency totals, cyclic hour and weekday features, and the fraction of
the month remaining complete the observation.

Current arrivals are known when scheduling; realized grid measurements are
available only through \(t-1\). The observation summarizes the available
information rather than exposing the simulator's full state.

\section{Actions and Feasibility}
\label{subsec:actions}

PPO outputs nine preferences in \([-6,6]\): four for service destinations,
one for batch release, and four for batch destinations. A decoder turns
these preferences into the executed schedule:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A softmax splits service among sites, and a projection keeps the
        allocation within each site's capacity.
  \item The release preference maps to a fraction of queued batch work.
        The decoder increases release when needed to meet deadlines and
        limits it to the remaining capacity.
  \item A softmax splits released batch work among destinations, subject to
        capacity. A global earliest-deadline-first queue selects the work.
\end{itemize}
PPO learns scheduling preferences; the decoder enforces service, deadline,
and capacity constraints. Feasibility therefore comes from the decoder,
without constraint penalties in the reward.

\section{Reward}
\label{subsec:reward}

The raw hourly reward follows the form of the joint objective from the
problem statement:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    r_t
    ={}&-\frac{0.5}{C_R}\sum_m I_{m,t}\\
    &-\frac{0.5}{C_Q}\sum_m\left[
      \left(M_{m,t}-M_{m,t-1}\right)
      -\left(O_{m,t}-O_{m,t-1}\right)
    \right],
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where \(I_{m,t}\) is ramp impact, and \(M_{m,t}\) and \(O_{m,t}\) are the
running normalized adjusted and original peaks. Each maximum is initialized
from the first decision hour; its first increment is measured from zero.
The preceding warm hour contributes to the first ramp but not to the peaks.

The peak increments sum to the month's peak impact, so the raw,
undiscounted episode return is \(\sum_t r_t=-J_e\). Lowering the joint score
therefore increases this return. Negative ramp impacts and positive hourly
rewards are valid.

Both reference scales use total-grid quantities under no flexibility,
as defined in the problem statement.

\chapter{TRAINING WITH PPO}
\label{sec:ppo}

PPO handles the decoder's continuous preference actions. We use its
Stable-Baselines3 implementation \cite{schulman2017ppo,raffin2021sb3} with an
actor--critic network: the policy chooses actions, while a separate value
function estimates discounted training returns
\cite{sutton2018reinforcement}.

\section{Policy Updates}

Each rollout collects 512 steps from each of four environments, or 2,048
transitions. The policy samples actions from a diagonal Gaussian with a
learned mean and per-dimension standard deviations. Samples are clipped to
\([-6,6]\) before reaching the decoder.

Generalized advantage estimation \cite{schulman2016gae} uses the rewards
and value estimates to assess which sampled actions did better than
expected. Estimates stop at the end of the rollout or month. At an
unfinished rollout, the value function estimates the remaining return;
at month-end, there is no future return. PPO then updates the policy using
its clipped objective. Clipping limits the incentive for large policy
changes; it does not impose a hard 20\% bound on action-probability changes.
Appendix~\ref{subsec:ppo-details} gives the update equations.

\section{Normalization and Discounting}

During training, observations are standardized using running means and
variances and clipped to \(\pm10\). Rewards are divided by a running
standard deviation of discounted returns and clipped to \(\pm10\).
The policy and value function train on these transformed values; reported
rewards and \(J_e\) use raw values.

PPO uses \(\gamma=0.99\), so training favors earlier rewards. It is an
approximate method for the monthly problem: evaluation uses the raw,
undiscounted objective, while training uses discounted, normalized rewards.

\section{Training Setup}

The policy and value networks each have two hidden layers of 64 tanh units,
with 19,603 trainable parameters in total. Table~\ref{tab:hyperparameters}
lists the settings. We have not tuned them for this problem.

In each fold, each seed trains a fresh policy on the fold's nine training
months. Each environment cycles through a shuffled order of these months
before repeating them. Everything fitted to data, including the forecasts,
the observation scaling, and \(C_R\) and \(C_Q\), comes from the same nine
months. The requested 1,000,000 interactions are rounded up to a checkpoint
boundary, giving 1,024,000 interactions per run.

Every fold uses seeds 4101--4103, so the cross-validation trains twelve
policies. A seed controls network initialization, action sampling, minibatch
order, and the order of training months. Data, constraints, and
hyperparameters stay the same within a fold, so differences between seeds
measure variation from training randomness, and differences between folds
add the effect of which months were held out.

\chapter{EVALUATION}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Each saved policy runs on each of its fold's three held-out months, one
continuous episode per month of 672 to 744 hours. It acts deterministically,
using the Gaussian mean instead of sampling. Observation normalization uses
frozen training statistics, and rewards are reported without normalization.
The no-flexibility baseline runs on the same hours, with the same arrivals,
grid data, and forecasts.

We report \(J_e(\mu_{\mathrm{NF}})-J_e(\mu)\), positive when PPO improves on
the baseline, for every held-out month and seed, and show the ramp and peak
contributions separately. The cross-validation estimate for a seed is its
mean improvement over the twelve held-out months, one per 2025 month; the
spread of these means across seeds measures training randomness. Regional
results report squared-ramp reductions relative to the original grid and
peak changes in MW relative to the no-flexibility fleet. These references
are labeled separately in the figures and tables.

We also report percentage reductions from no flexibility in total squared
normalized ramps, summed normalized regional peaks, and the fleet's added
peak impact. Each percentage divides the reduction by the corresponding
no-flexibility value. The signed joint score is reported as an absolute
improvement.

For each final policy and held-out month, we also recomputed \(J_e\) from the
raw grid panel and site-power trajectory. The largest difference from the
reported score was \(5\times10^{-17}\). All 36 evaluations finished with no
service, deadline, or capacity violation.

The forecast inputs were chosen by the same four-fold cross-validation, and
the forecast lead times were set earlier in development; no choice in this
document used the test month. The cross-validation scores each policy on
months whose workload it has seen in training, with new grid data. The test
month adds unseen workload and a larger fleet and will be scored once, with
the final policies.

\section{How the Policies Act}

The policies mainly move service between sites: 29\% to 34\% of each month's
service work changes sites. Batch work waits 0.8 to 1.3 hours on average
within its 24-hour window, and the decoder adjusts the requested schedule in
66\% to 83\% of hours to meet capacity and deadline requirements.

Fig.~\ref{fig:isone} shows ISO-NE over three days around its May peak hour,
under the fold-1 policy of seed 4102. Under the baseline, site power stays
between 272 and 305 MW. The policy runs the site near full power, about
400 MW, while net load is low and cuts it to about 167 MW as net load climbs
to its evening peak. At the peak hour it draws 167 MW, 129 MW less than the
baseline.

\chapter{LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS}
\label{sec:limitations}

The twelve policies improve on no flexibility in every 2025 month they did
not train on. Every month replays the same workload, so cross-validation
tests new grid months only. The test month, with unseen workload and a larger
fleet, will be scored once by the final ten-seed campaign (seeds 4101--4110),
trained on all twelve months.

The next comparison is a simple forecast-aware rule using the same
observations, workload constraints, and feasibility decoder. That comparison
would help determine what learning adds beyond flexible scheduling. No
heuristic results are included here.

\begin{samepage}
We have not yet compared ramp-only and peak-only policies. Those runs would
help separate the objectives' effects. Routing remains free and
instantaneous, making the available geographic flexibility optimistic.

\end{samepage}

\section{PPO Update Equations}
\label{subsec:ppo-details}

The policy \(\pi_\theta(a\mid o)\) describes the distribution of sampled actions;
\(V_\phi(o)\) estimates discounted training returns. Here \(o_t\) is the
normalized observation and \(\widetilde r_t\) is the normalized and clipped
training reward. Generalized advantage estimation uses
\begin{align}
  \delta_t
  &=\widetilde r_t+\gamma V_\phi(o_{t+1})-V_\phi(o_t),
  \label{eq:td-error}\\
  \hat A_t
  &=\sum_{l=0}^{K_t-1}(\gamma\lambda)^l\delta_{t+l},
  \label{eq:gae}
\end{align}
where \(K_t\) is the number of collected steps from \(t\) to the earlier of
the rollout boundary or month-end. At month-end the next-state value is
zero. At an unfinished rollout boundary it supplies the bootstrap value.
Every run uses \(\gamma=0.99\) and \(\lambda=0.95\).

Advantages are standardized within each minibatch before the policy update.
For the sampled action \(a_t\), define
\(\rho_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta(a_t\mid o_t)/
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_t\mid o_t)\). PPO maximizes
\begin{equation}
  L^{\mathrm{clip}}(\theta)
  =\mathbb E_t\!\left[
    \min\!\left(
      \rho_t\hat A_t,\;
      \mathrm{clip}(\rho_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A_t
    \right)
  \right],
  \label{eq:ppo-clip}
\end{equation}
with \(\epsilon=0.2\). The probability ratio uses the sampled Gaussian
action; the environment receives its clipped version. Clipping limits the
incentive for large policy changes; it does not enforce a bound on the
policy itself.

The value network fits estimated returns with squared error, weighted 0.5
in the combined loss. There is no entropy bonus, and gradients are clipped
to norm 0.5. These settings are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparameters}.

\section{Training and Checkpoints (Chapter~\ref{sec:ppo})}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Each fold uses its own \(C_R\) and \(C_Q\), means over its nine
        training months under no flexibility of total squared adjusted ramps
        and summed normalized adjusted peaks: \(C_R\) from 5.853 to 6.613 and
        \(C_Q\) from 3.576 to 3.739. The final policies will use
        \(C_R=6.102\) and \(C_Q=3.653\). All folds use the capacity-sized
        training fleet and least-squares forecasts fitted on the fold's
        training months, recorded as forecast vintage
        \codepath{causal-least-squares-v4+cv-kX}.
  \item \codepath{run_training} in \codepath{ramp_rl/runner.py} reads the
        PPO settings from \codepath{env/protocols/four_market_v2.yaml}.
        Stable-Baselines3 2.9.0 is pinned in \codepath{requirements.txt},
        and the runner checks the installed version.
  \item \codepath{VecNormalize} standardizes and clips observations and
        scales and clips training rewards. Its statistics are saved in
        \codepath{vecnormalize.pkl}. Evaluation freezes those statistics
        and disables reward normalization.
  \item Four environment copies step in turn within one training process.
        Each rollout provides 2,048 transitions. Ten epochs with minibatches
        of 256 give 80 gradient steps per update; a run's 1,024,000
        interactions give 500 updates.
  \item \codepath{FourMarketV2WindowEnv._next_window_id} in
        \codepath{energy_model_v3/four_market_v2.py} draws a fold's training
        months in shuffled cycles. The runner seeds Python, NumPy, PyTorch,
        PPO, and the environment copies to control training randomness.
  \item \codepath{TrainingCallback} writes raw metrics to
        \codepath{learning_curve.csv} after each rollout.
        \codepath{save_latest_checkpoint} saves a resumable checkpoint every
        25 rollouts, or 51,200 interactions. Each run evaluates milestones
        at 153,600, 512,000, and 1,024,000 interactions.
  \item \codepath{environment_identity} in \codepath{ramp_rl/contract.py}
        records the objective, input digest, and observation and action
        shapes. Resume checks also compare PPO settings and seed. The
        evaluator rejects checkpoints with incompatible environment
        identities. \codepath{tests/ramp_v6/test_campaign_instrumentation.py}
        tests these checks and checkpoint resumption.
\end{itemize}
On the eight-core laptop used here, six runs trained at a time and each took
55 to 63 minutes; the whole cross-validation took 2.0 hours.

\section{Evaluation and Figures
  (Chapters~\ref{sec:evaluation}--\ref{sec:results})}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \codepath{evaluate_checkpoint} and \codepath{_run_episode} in
        \codepath{ramp_rl/evaluation.py} run the deterministic policy and
        no-flexibility baseline. They report raw joint scores, component
        outcomes, regional metrics, and feasibility checks.
  \item \codepath{scripts/run_four_market_cv.py} trains every (fold, seed)
        pair and evaluates each saved milestone on each of the fold's
        held-out months. Summaries and learning curves are under
        \codepath{output/four_market_joint_v3/cv/cv-trace-clock-1m/}, with
        \codepath{cv.json} collecting every month's improvement; checkpoints
        are under \codepath{models/four_market_joint_v3/cv/cv-trace-clock-1m/}.
  \item \codepath{scripts/build_ppo_cv_figures.py} replays each final policy
        and the baseline on every held-out month, recomputes \(J_e\) from the
        trajectories, draws the figures, and writes their supporting values to
        \codepath{docs/figures/ppo_cv/figure_data.json}. Squared-ramp
        percentages in the regional figure use the original grid's
        squared-ramp total. Table~\ref{tab:nf-percentages} instead compares
        each final policy with no flexibility, month by month; its values
        are stored under \codepath{no_flexibility_percentage_reductions}. The
        ramp denominator is the baseline's total squared adjusted ramps, the
        peak denominator is its sum of normalized adjusted regional peaks,
        and the added-peak denominator is
        \(\sum_m\Phi_{m,e}(\mu_{\mathrm{NF}})\).
  \item The service-reallocation diagnostic uses differences between
        destination allocations and origin arrivals. It measures the
        minimum work that must have changed sites, rather than tracking
        individual service jobs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproduction Commands}
From the repository root, run the following commands in PowerShell:
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/build_four_market_v2_factory.py
python scripts/run_four_market_cv.py `
    --seeds 4101 4102 4103 --timesteps 1000000 `
    --tag cv-trace-clock-1m --workers 6
python scripts/build_ppo_cv_figures.py --tag cv-trace-clock-1m
\end{verbatim}
}
The cross-validation script runs each (fold, seed) pair in its own process.
The option
\codepath{--ramp-weight} selects ramp-only (1), equal-weight (0.5), or
peak-only (0) objectives. The planned ten-seed campaign is run by
\codepath{scripts/run_four_market_v2_campaign.py}.
